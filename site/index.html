The sheet demo and uploaded OBJ/self-contained GLB triangle meshes run through the cloth-lab Rust core compiled to WebAssembly. Mesh density changes the sheet topology while preserving its physical size. Textile presets map to solver compliance and contact parameters; solver controls change the explicit fixed-step configuration. Self-collision is opt-in so historical comparisons remain stable; when enabled, the configured thickness is solved by the same deterministic Rust vertex/triangle kernel inside every triangle-mesh solver iteration. Pins are persistent kinematic constraints whose targets can be moved. Collision scenery is an explicit validated Rust input: it can be disabled or configured as a sphere or x-axis capsule, with accessible position/size controls and direct pointer dragging. Camera orbit, zoom, and pan remain renderer-only inspection state; cloth, pin, and obstacle dragging use the same current camera basis so view changes do not alter interaction meaning. The inspector reads authoritative topology, constraint, error, contact, self-collision, obstacle, mannequin-collider, and normalized-asset evidence from Rust; wireframe and normal overlays are rendering-only views of authoritative positions and triangles. Built-in garment templates are deterministic drape fixtures rather than inferred sewing patterns. Mannequin pose sampling, quaternion interpolation, and parent/child transform evaluation reuse the pinned renderer-independent three-d-animation contract from 3d-lab; Cloth Lab remains responsible for adapting those joint transforms into collision primitives and garment attachments. Uploaded mesh formats still provide geometry only: seams, patterns, measured fabric properties, skins, and animation are not inferred when unsupported by the source.
